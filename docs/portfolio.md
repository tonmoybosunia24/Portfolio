# Portfolio

The home page (`/`) is the portfolio. `app/page.tsx` only renders `views/home/index.tsx`, which lists the sections in page order. Each section is a file in `views/home/sections/` (`HeroSection`, `ServicesSection`, …), and the parts only those sections use (`ContactForm`, `HexPattern`, `SkillIcon`, `TestimonialSlider`, `Typewriter`) are in `views/home/components/`. Each project also has its own page at `/work/<slug>` (see [Project pages](#project-pages)), and there is a blog at `/blog` (see [Blog](#blog)).

Every page uses `components/layout/SiteShell.tsx`: the header, the footer, the card glow (`GlowTracker`) and the scroll reveals (`ScrollReveal`). Those parts live in `components/layout/`, with `PageHero`, the top of an inner page on the home hero's background. Shared pieces such as the icons, `SectionHeading`, `ProjectBanner` and the blog cards are in `components/ui/`; the parts of a long write-up (`ArticleLayout`, `StoryBlock`, `TableOfContents`, `ContactCta`, `Breadcrumb`) are in `components/article/`. The header links (`NAV_LINKS`: Home, Services, Experience, Projects, Blog, Contact) are Next.js links. Projects and Blog open their own pages (`/work`, `/blog`); the others point to `/#<section>`, so they work from any page, as do the footer links. `components/layout/use-active-nav.ts` picks the current link, shown in pink: on the home page the section in view (each link's `section`; the last one whose top has passed 35% of the screen, or Contact at the very bottom, `aria-current="location"`), and on other pages the page link whose `href` starts the address, such as Projects on `/work/crave` (`aria-current="page"`). The header is fixed to the top of the screen on every page: see-through at the top, and after 50px of scrolling (`SCROLL_OFFSET`) it fades in a blurred background and a bottom border.

## Content

Text, links and contact details live in `constants/`, one file per area:

| File                     | Holds                                                                                                          |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `profile.ts`             | `PROFILE` (name, hero text), `SIDEBAR` (side panel and contact details), `SOCIALS`                             |
| `navigation.ts`          | `NAV_LINKS` (header) and `FOOTER`                                                                              |
| `contact.ts`             | `CONTACT`, the contact section and the email sent from it                                                      |
| `home/*.ts`              | One file per home page section: `services`, `achievements`, `skills`, `expertise`, `education`, `testimonials` |
| `projects.ts`, `blog.ts` | The projects and the blog posts                                                                                |

Their types (`Project`, `BlogPost`, the icon names) are in `types/` and imported from `@/types`, so the files in `constants/` hold only content. Values marked `TODO` are placeholders. Images are in `public/images/`.

## Theme

Colors and fonts are defined in `app/globals.css` and used as Tailwind classes. The neutrals follow GitHub's colours, in both themes:

| Class                                        | Value                                                                                                                       |
| -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `primary`                                    | Accent `#ff014f` (was `pink-primary`)                                                                                       |
| `background`, `foreground`                   | Page and text: `#0d1117` / `#f0f6fc` dark, `#fff` / `#1f2328` light                                                         |
| `gray-light`                                 | Secondary text: `#b1bac4` dark, `#59636e` light                                                                             |
| `black-soft`, `dark`                         | Panels and cards: `#151b23`, `#161b22` dark, `#f6f8fa` light                                                                |
| `white-overlay`, `line`                      | Subtle fills and borders                                                                                                    |
| `feature-start`, `feature-end`, `feature-fg` | The highlight card (years of experience, "Have an app idea?"): crimson with white text dark, soft pink with dark text light |
| `font-sans` (default) / `font-rubik`         | Rajdhani for headings, Rubik for body copy                                                                                  |

`section` is the page-width container every section uses, and `round-icon` the round icon button of the menu, theme switch and social links (size it with `size-*`). Buttons get the hand cursor from a base rule in `app/globals.css` (Tailwind 4 gives them the arrow), unless they are disabled. `text-stroke` draws outlined text. `glow-card` gives a card a pink border glow on hover that follows the pointer (`components/layout/GlowTracker.tsx` sets its position); the card needs a border, transparent is fine. `animate-float` and `animate-blink` are off for visitors who prefer reduced motion, and so is the typewriter.

Section headings (a small pink label and a one-line title) use `components/ui/SectionHeading.tsx`, so every section below the hero looks the same. The services section (`views/home/sections/ServicesSection.tsx`, content in `SERVICES`) shows six numbered cards: one per row on phones, two from 768px and three from 992px. The achievements section (`views/home/sections/AchievementsSection.tsx`, content in `ACHIEVEMENTS`) shows the years of experience in a large card and four numbers in a 2×2 grid beside it, or below it under 992px. The skills section (`views/home/sections/SkillsSection.tsx`, content in `SKILLS`) shows chips in three rows that slide sideways (`animate-marquee`, the middle row `animate-marquee-reverse`) and stop on hover; screen readers get one plain list. Logos are Simple Icons paths in `views/home/components/SkillIcon.tsx`; a new icon name goes in `SkillIconName` too. The expertise section (`views/home/sections/ExpertiseSection.tsx`, content in `EXPERTISE`) shows two groups of skills with a level bar each; the experience and education section (`views/home/sections/EducationSection.tsx`, content in `EDUCATION.groups`) shows an "Experience" group, then an "Education" group, each a heading and two-column cards; the bars fill in one after another when they come on screen (`reveal-grow`, started by `ScrollReveal`) and are simply full without JavaScript or with reduced motion. The projects section (`views/home/sections/ProjectsSection.tsx`, content in `PROJECTS`) shows each project as a framed 4:3 banner, a dark one for dark mode and a light one for light mode (`banner.dark`, `banner.light`; the `light:` variant in `app/globals.css` switches them), 2400×1800 in `public/images/projects/` ( give an updated banner a new file name, as the image cache keeps the old one), then its title, tech and an arrow button. The light banners show the app name in the site's pink and two iPhone mockups in a light studio; the dark ones follow the project cards of the Reeni template: a black panel with a crimson glow, "App For" and the app's purpose in pink at the top left, and two large phones cut off by the bottom edge. The dark banners are designed in Figma (the `Banner/<App>/Dark Reeni` frames) and exported at 2x. The projects (Crave, Precise Clock, Amar Seba, Glacial Rental, ClassyShop, Vehicle Log) use banners designed in Figma and exported at 2400×1800, one banner for both themes (`banner.light` and `banner.dark` point at the same `*-banner.webp`). A banner exported again gets a new name (`*-banner-v2.webp`), so the image cache never serves the old one. They are 2400×1800 and served at quality 95 (`images.qualities` in `next.config.ts`) so small text stays sharp. Without screenshots the phones show a screen drawn in code. With an `href`, the whole card links to it. The testimonials section (`views/home/sections/TestimonialsSection.tsx` and `TestimonialSlider.tsx`, content in `TESTIMONIALS`) is an endless slider: the list is drawn three times and jumps back to the middle copy when it settles, it moves on every 5 seconds (paused on hover or focus, off for reduced motion), it can be dragged with the mouse (snapping is off during the drag, and a drag of 60px or more moves on at least one review), and screen readers get only the middle copy. The reviews and avatars (DiceBear Notionists, CC0) are samples to replace with real client feedback; until then the section is hidden (`TESTIMONIALS.visible` is false, and the footer links to the experience section instead). The blogs section (`views/home/sections/BlogsSection.tsx`, `id="blogs"`, posts in `constants/blog.ts`) shows the three newest posts as cards (`components/ui/BlogCard.tsx`): the cover photo (`BlogCover`) with the category on it, the date and reading time (`utils/format-date.ts`, `utils/reading-time.ts`, 200 words a minute), the title, an excerpt and "Read Article"; the whole card opens `/blog/<slug>`. Blog cards lift on hover but have no pink border glow. One card per row on phones, two from 768px (the third hidden), three from 992px. A "View All Posts" button links to `/blog`. Posts use the same blocks as project write-ups (`ContentBlock`). The contact section (`views/home/sections/ContactSection.tsx` and `ContactForm.tsx`, content in `CONTACT`) shows the details from `SIDEBAR.contacts` and a form; name, email and message are required, checked in the browser and again by the server action `sendContactMessage` (`app/actions.ts`), which formats the email using `buildContactEmail` (`utils/contact-email.ts`) and emails it to `EMAIL_USER` with Nodemailer through Gmail (`utils/mailer.server.ts`), the visitor's address as reply-to. A hidden `website` field catches bots. Without `EMAIL_USER` and `EMAIL_PASSWORD` (see [environment-variables.md](environment-variables.md)) the form says sending failed and gives the email address.

The hero background is drawn in code (`views/home/components/HexPattern.tsx`, also used faintly behind the project previews, and `views/home/components/LightBeams.tsx`), not an image, so it stays sharp at every size and stays lined up with the portrait.

## Animations

All animations are CSS in `app/globals.css`, with no animation library (one small observer starts the scroll reveals), and are off for visitors who prefer reduced motion. The hero text rises in once on load (`animate-intro`, `intro-delay-1` and `-2`). Switching between dark and light mode, the new theme spreads out in a circle from the toggle button (a view transition in `ThemeToggle`; browsers without view transitions switch at once). Below it, content fades and slides into place when it scrolls into view: section headings, cards and the footer columns rise (`reveal`), the achievement, expertise and contact columns come in from the sides from 992px (`reveal-left`, `reveal-right`) and the project cards grow slightly (`reveal-zoom`); below 992px these rise too. Cards side by side start one after the other: `revealDelay(index, columns)` in `lib/reveal.ts` gives the `reveal-delay-*` class. Each reveal plays once, in under a second, when the element comes on screen: `components/layout/ScrollReveal.tsx` (an IntersectionObserver) marks the page `reveal-ready`, which hides that content, and adds `is-revealed` to each element as it appears, including elements added after the page loaded (a MutationObserver watches for them), so nothing stays hidden. Without JavaScript the content simply shows. A pink bar under the header shows how far down the page the visitor is (`scroll-progress`, scroll-driven, so it does not show in browsers without scroll-driven animations). The page wrapper clips horizontal overflow (`overflow-x-clip`), so content sliding in from the side never adds a horizontal scrollbar.

## Project pages

`app/work/[slug]/page.tsx` builds one page per item of `PROJECTS.items` ahead of time (`generateStaticParams`); any other slug is a 404 (`dynamicParams = false`). The page title is the project name and your full name, and the description is its `summary`. The project cards on the home page link to these pages, and `app/sitemap.ts` lists them.
`app/work/page.tsx` renders `views/work/index.tsx`: a `PageHero` with a breadcrumb (Home / Projects) and `PROJECTS.page` (label, title, description), then every project as a card. Tabs above the cards (`views/work/components/ProjectTabs.tsx`, a client component, labels in `PROJECTS.tabs`) show all projects, only the mobile apps, the web apps or the UI/UX designs, by each project's `kind` (`mobile`, `web` or `design`). Below 640px the tabs show short labels (All, Mobile, Web, UI/UX; `short` in `PROJECTS.tabs`) so all four fit on a phone. They are plain text on the left, in line with the title and cards, with a pink bar sliding under the selected tab (no line under the others) and the counts after the labels from 640px; the arrow keys move between tabs. The home page's projects section shows only the `featured` projects and a "View All Projects" button to `/work`. Both use `components/ui/ProjectGrid.tsx` and `ProjectCard.tsx` (the card's heading is `h3` on the home page and `h2` on `/work`); the "View All" links below the projects and blog sections are `components/ui/ViewAllLink.tsx`: the text and a round arrow between two thin lines that fade out, like the section labels, with no box (pink on hover).

The page is `views/work/detail/index.tsx`, built one section at a time in `views/work/detail/sections/`:

- `ProjectHeroSection`: in a `PageHero`, a breadcrumb (Home / Projects / the project, where Projects leads to `/work`), the category, the title, the `summary`, the `tech` as small chips and the `links` as buttons (the first one pink) on the left, the framed banner on the right, both starting at the top. Stacked below 992px.
- `ProjectFactsSection`: the `facts` (role, client, year, duration, platforms) as five small cards styled like the service cards: a round icon (`FactIconSvg` in `components/ui/icons.tsx`) that turns pink on hover, the label, the value and a faint outlined number in the corner. Two per row on phones, three from 768px, five from 992px.
- `ProjectStorySection`: the `story` in `components/article/ArticleLayout.tsx`, the full write-up read like a blog post, in a column at most 760px wide. Its blocks are headings, paragraphs, lists and pull quotes (`ContentBlock` in `types/content.ts`, drawn by `StoryBlock`). From 992px a sidebar runs beside it: a "Have an app idea?" card linking to the contact section at its top, beside the start of the text, then "On this page", which lists the headings (each gets an id from `utils/slugify.ts`) and stays in view while scrolling. Below 992px only the card follows the text.

Projects live in `constants/projects.ts` (`PROJECTS`, `findProject`). Each has a `slug` (its address), `kind` (`mobile`, `web` or `design`), `featured`, `summary`, `facts`, `tech`, `links` and `story`. All are real: three Flutter apps, Glacial Rental (Laravel and React), ClassyShop and Vehicle Log, a mobile app design made in Figma. The home page features the three Flutter apps and Glacial Rental; ClassyShop and Vehicle Log are only on `/work`. A project without `links` shows no buttons.

## 404 page

`app/not-found.tsx` renders `views/not-found/index.tsx` (text in `constants/not-found.ts`) for unknown addresses and `notFound()`, with the header and footer of every page. On the inner pages' background, a large "404" (the 4s outlined, the pink 0 floating) sits above the "Page not found" heading, a short message, Back to Home and View Projects buttons, and the other header links. It fits one screen on a 1280×600 laptop (`compact`).

## Blog

Posts live in `constants/blog.ts` (`BLOG`, `sortedPosts`, `findPost`). Each has a `slug` (its address), `title`, `excerpt`, `date` (`YYYY-MM-DD`, newest first), `category`, an `image` (the cover: a 4:3 webp, 1600×1200, in `public/images/blog/` with its `alt` text and, for an Unsplash photo, the photographer's `credit`), optional `links` (buttons below the title) and its `content`, in the same blocks as a project's write-up. Besides headings, paragraphs, lists and quotes, content can hold a `gallery` of phone screenshots (two per row on phones, four from 576px) and `code` (a monospaced block that scrolls sideways when a line is too long). The first post, about the Flutter Architecture Template, is a full walkthrough written from the template's README and `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, with code blocks and no screenshots; the other two (publishing on Google Play, Flutter performance with GetX) are written from the client apps. All three covers (`*-banner-v2.webp`) are 4:3 banners in the same style, designed in Figma and exported at 1600×1200. A cover from Unsplash would need a `credit`, which the post page shows below it. To add a cover, save it as a 1600×1200 webp (4:3) in `public/images/blog/` with a new file name, as the image cache keeps an old image under the same name.

- `/blog` (`app/blog/page.tsx`, `views/blog/index.tsx`): a `PageHero` with the breadcrumb, the label, the title and the description, then every post as a card (`components/ui/BlogGrid.tsx`).
- `/blog/<slug>` (`app/blog/[slug]/page.tsx`, `views/blog/detail/`): one page per post, built ahead of time; any other slug is a 404. `PostHeroSection` shows the breadcrumb (Home / Blog / the post), the category, the title, the excerpt, the author with the date and reading time and the `links` on the left, and the framed cover with its photo credit on the right; then the article in `ArticleLayout`, with the same "On this page" sidebar as a project; then `MorePostsSection`, the three newest other posts.

The page title is the post's title and your full name, and the description is its `excerpt`. `app/sitemap.ts` lists `/blog` and every post.

## Sizes

Header and hero sizes follow the Reeni template. Tailwind's breakpoints are set to match it in `app/globals.css`: `sm` 576px, `md` 768px, `lg` 992px, `xl` 1200px, `2xl` 1400px and `3xl` 1600px. The `section` container gives the same content widths (345px on a 375px phone, then 510, 705, 945, 1125 and at most 1275px).

## Viewing on a phone

With `npm run dev` running, open the **Network** address it prints (e.g. `http://192.168.1.102:3000`) on a phone on the same Wi-Fi. `allowedDevOrigins` in `next.config.ts` allows `192.168.*.*` so the page's scripts load there too. If the phone can't connect, allow Node.js through the Windows firewall for private networks.
