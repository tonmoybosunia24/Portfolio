# Portfolio

The home page (`/`) is the portfolio. It is built one section at a time in `views/home/`; `app/page.tsx` only renders `views/home/Home.tsx`. Each project also has its own page at `/work/<slug>` (see [Project pages](#project-pages)), and there is a blog at `/blog` (see [Blog](#blog)).

Every page uses `components/layout/SiteShell.tsx`: the header, the footer, the card glow (`GlowTracker`) and the scroll reveals (`ScrollReveal`). Those parts live in `components/layout/`, with `PageHero`, the top of an inner page on the home hero's background. Shared pieces such as the icons, `SectionHeading`, `ProjectBanner` and the blog cards are in `components/ui/`; the parts of a long write-up (`ArticleLayout`, `StoryBlock`, `TableOfContents`, `ContactCta`, `Breadcrumb`) are in `components/article/`. Menu and footer links point to `/#<section>`, so they work from any page. The header sticks once the page's top section, marked `data-page-hero`, has scrolled away.

## Content

Text, links and contact details live in `constants/profile.ts`. Values marked `TODO` are placeholders. Images are in `public/images/`.

## Theme

Colors and fonts are defined in `app/globals.css` and used as Tailwind classes:

| Class                                | Value                                      |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| `pink-primary`                       | Accent `#ff014f`                           |
| `gray-light`                         | Secondary text `#c2c2c2`                   |
| `black-soft`, `dark`                 | Panels `#151515`, `#141414`                |
| `white-overlay`, `line`              | Subtle fills and borders                   |
| `font-sans` (default) / `font-rubik` | Rajdhani for headings, Rubik for body copy |

`section` is the page-width container every section uses. Buttons get the hand cursor from a base rule in `app/globals.css` (Tailwind 4 gives them the arrow), unless they are disabled. `text-stroke` draws outlined text. `glow-card` gives a card a pink border glow on hover that follows the pointer (`components/layout/GlowTracker.tsx` sets its position); the card needs a border, transparent is fine. `animate-float` and `animate-blink` are off for visitors who prefer reduced motion, and so is the typewriter.

Section headings (a small pink label and a one-line title) use `components/ui/SectionHeading.tsx`, so every section below the hero looks the same. The services section (`views/home/Services.tsx`, content in `SERVICES`) shows six numbered cards: one per row on phones, two from 768px and three from 992px. The achievements section (`views/home/Achievements.tsx`, content in `ACHIEVEMENTS`) shows the years of experience in a large card and four numbers in a 2×2 grid beside it, or below it under 992px. The skills section (`views/home/Skills.tsx`, content in `SKILLS`) shows chips in three rows that slide sideways (`animate-marquee`, the middle row `animate-marquee-reverse`) and stop on hover; screen readers get one plain list. Logos are Simple Icons paths in `views/home/SkillIcon.tsx`; a new icon name goes in `SkillIconName` too. The expertise section (`views/home/Expertise.tsx`, content in `EXPERTISE`) shows two groups of skills with a level bar each; the bars fill in one after another when they come on screen (`reveal-grow`, started by `ScrollReveal`) and are simply full without JavaScript or with reduced motion. The projects section (`views/home/Projects.tsx`, content in `PROJECTS`) shows each project as a framed 4:3 banner, a dark one for dark mode and a light one for light mode (`banner.dark`, `banner.light`; the `light:` variant in `app/globals.css` switches them), 2400×1800 in `public/images/projects/` ( give an updated banner a new file name, as the image cache keeps the old one), then its title, tech and an arrow button. The light banners show the app name in the site's pink and two iPhone mockups in a light studio; the dark ones follow the project cards of the Reeni template: a black panel with a crimson glow, "App For" and the app's purpose in pink at the top left, and two large phones cut off by the bottom edge. The dark banners are designed in Figma (the `Banner/<App>/Dark Reeni` frames) and exported at 2x. The four projects (Crave, time tracking, Amar Seba, ClassyShop) use banners designed in Figma and exported at 2400×1800, one banner for both themes (`banner.light` and `banner.dark` point at the same `*-banner.webp`). They are 2400×1800 and served at quality 95 (`images.qualities` in `next.config.ts`) so small text stays sharp. Without screenshots the phones show a screen drawn in code. With an `href`, the whole card links to it. The testimonials section (`views/home/Testimonials.tsx` and `TestimonialSlider.tsx`, content in `TESTIMONIALS`) is an endless slider: the list is drawn three times and jumps back to the middle copy when it settles, it moves on every 5 seconds (paused on hover or focus, off for reduced motion), it can be dragged with the mouse (snapping is off during the drag, and a drag of 60px or more moves on at least one review), and screen readers get only the middle copy. The reviews and avatars (DiceBear Notionists, CC0) are samples to replace with real client feedback. The blogs section (`views/home/Blogs.tsx`, `id="blogs"`, posts in `constants/blog.ts`) shows the three newest posts as cards (`components/ui/BlogCard.tsx`): the cover photo (`BlogCover`) with the category on it, the date and reading time (`utils/format-date.ts`, `utils/reading-time.ts`, 200 words a minute), the title, an excerpt and "Read Article"; the whole card opens `/blog/<slug>`. Blog cards lift on hover but have no pink border glow. One card per row on phones, two from 768px (the third hidden), three from 992px. A "View All Posts" button links to `/blog`. Posts use the same blocks as project write-ups (`ContentBlock`); the current four are samples. The contact section (`views/home/Contact.tsx` and `ContactForm.tsx`, content in `CONTACT`) shows the details from `SIDEBAR.contacts` and a form; name, email and message are required, and as there is no server yet, sending opens the visitor's email app with the message filled in.

The hero background is drawn in code (`views/home/HexPattern.tsx`, also used faintly behind the project previews, and `LightBeams` in `views/home/Hero.tsx`), not an image, so it stays sharp at every size and stays lined up with the portrait.

## Animations

All animations are CSS in `app/globals.css`, with no animation library (one small observer starts the scroll reveals), and are off for visitors who prefer reduced motion. The hero text rises in once on load (`animate-intro`, `intro-delay-1` and `-2`). Below it, content fades and slides into place when it scrolls into view: section headings, cards and the footer columns rise (`reveal`), the achievement, expertise and contact columns come in from the sides from 992px (`reveal-left`, `reveal-right`) and the project cards grow slightly (`reveal-zoom`); below 992px these rise too. Cards side by side start one after the other: `revealDelay(index, columns)` in `lib/reveal.ts` gives the `reveal-delay-*` class. Each reveal plays once, in under a second, when the element comes on screen: `components/layout/ScrollReveal.tsx` (an IntersectionObserver) marks the page `reveal-ready`, which hides that content, and adds `is-revealed` to each element as it appears, including elements added after the page loaded (a MutationObserver watches for them), so nothing stays hidden. Without JavaScript the content simply shows. A pink bar under the header shows how far down the page the visitor is (`scroll-progress`, scroll-driven, so it does not show in browsers without scroll-driven animations). The page wrapper clips horizontal overflow (`overflow-x-clip`), so content sliding in from the side never adds a horizontal scrollbar.

## Project pages

`app/work/[slug]/page.tsx` builds one page per item of `PROJECTS.items` ahead of time (`generateStaticParams`); any other slug is a 404 (`dynamicParams = false`). The page title is the project name and your full name, and the description is its `summary`. The project cards on the home page link to these pages, and `app/sitemap.ts` lists them.

The page is `views/work/detail/index.tsx`, built one section at a time in `views/work/detail/sections/`:

- `ProjectHeroSection`: in a `PageHero`, a breadcrumb (Home / Projects / the project), the category, the title, the `summary`, the `tech` as small chips and the `links` as buttons (the first one pink) on the left, the framed banner on the right, both starting at the top. Stacked below 992px.
- `ProjectFactsSection`: the `facts` (role, client, year, duration, platforms) as five small cards styled like the service cards: a round icon (`FactIconSvg` in `components/ui/icons.tsx`) that turns pink on hover, the label, the value and a faint outlined number in the corner. Two per row on phones, three from 768px, five from 992px.
- `ProjectStorySection`: the `story` in `components/article/ArticleLayout.tsx`, the full write-up read like a blog post, in a column at most 760px wide. Its blocks are headings, paragraphs, lists and pull quotes (`ContentBlock` in `types/content.ts`, drawn by `StoryBlock`). From 992px a sidebar runs beside it: a "Have an app idea?" card linking to the contact section at its top, beside the start of the text, then "On this page", which lists the headings (each gets an id from `utils/slugify.ts`) and stays in view while scrolling. Below 992px only the card follows the text.

Projects live in `constants/projects.ts` (`PROJECTS`, `findProject`). Each has a `slug` (its address), `summary`, `facts`, `tech`, `links` and `story`. All four are your projects (ClassyShop is a web app, the others are mobile apps); their facts and tech are marked TODO to check, and their links are empty until you add store, live site or repository pages (no buttons show without them).

## Blog

Posts live in `constants/blog.ts` (`BLOG`, `sortedPosts`, `findPost`). Each has a `slug` (its address), `title`, `excerpt`, `date` (`YYYY-MM-DD`, newest first), `category`, an `image` (the cover: a 16:9 webp, 1600×900, in `public/images/blog/` with its `alt` text and, for an Unsplash photo, the photographer's `credit`), optional `links` (buttons below the title) and its `content`, in the same blocks as a project's write-up. Besides headings, paragraphs, lists and quotes, content can hold a `gallery` of phone screenshots (two per row on phones, four from 576px) and `code` (a monospaced block that scrolls sideways when a line is too long). The first post, about the Flutter Architecture Template, uses both, with the template's own screenshots in `public/images/blog/flutter-architecture-template/`; the other four are samples to replace with your own articles. The template post's cover is your own banner; the other covers are photos from Unsplash, free to use under the Unsplash License, and the post page credits the photographer below the cover (posts without a `credit` show none). To add a cover, save it as a 1600×900 webp (16:9) in `public/images/blog/` with a new file name, as the image cache keeps an old image under the same name.

- `/blog` (`app/blog/page.tsx`, `views/blog/index.tsx`): a `PageHero` with the breadcrumb, the label, the title and the description, then every post as a card (`components/ui/BlogGrid.tsx`).
- `/blog/<slug>` (`app/blog/[slug]/page.tsx`, `views/blog/detail/`): one page per post, built ahead of time; any other slug is a 404. `PostHeroSection` shows the breadcrumb (Home / Blog / the post), the category, the title, the excerpt, the author with the date and reading time and the `links` on the left, and the framed cover with its photo credit on the right; then the article in `ArticleLayout`, with the same "On this page" sidebar as a project; then `MorePostsSection`, the three newest other posts.

The page title is the post's title and your full name, and the description is its `excerpt`. `app/sitemap.ts` lists `/blog` and every post.

## Sizes

Header and hero sizes follow the Reeni template. Tailwind's breakpoints are set to match it in `app/globals.css`: `sm` 576px, `md` 768px, `lg` 992px, `xl` 1200px, `2xl` 1400px and `3xl` 1600px. The `section` container gives the same content widths (345px on a 375px phone, then 510, 705, 945, 1125 and at most 1275px).

## Viewing on a phone

With `npm run dev` running, open the **Network** address it prints (e.g. `http://192.168.1.102:3000`) on a phone on the same Wi-Fi. `allowedDevOrigins` in `next.config.ts` allows `192.168.*.*` so the page's scripts load there too. If the phone can't connect, allow Node.js through the Windows firewall for private networks.
